# Six Months to Piano — Curriculum Scope

A daily self-study piano course for a middle-aged absolute beginner practicing
20–30 minutes a day. Each lesson is a printable PDF (explanation + engraved
exercises for both hands) plus an MP3 play-along.

This document covers **Month One (Days 1–30)** — the lessons that exist or are
currently being generated by `make_curriculum.py`. Months 2–6 will be scoped
after Month One is reviewed.

## Practice melodies: famous music at learner level (optional)

Most lessons use simple original exercises and melodies written for that day's
skill — that is the norm. **Where a famous theme happens to fit naturally**,
the practice melody may be a **simplified famous theme** (all public domain).
It is a bonus, never a requirement, and many lessons have none. When used,
these are practice arrangements, not performance editions — simplified
ruthlessly to what the learner knows that day:

- five-finger positions only, one note at a time per hand
- only note values and techniques introduced up to that day
- transposed, narrowed in range, ornaments dropped, rhythms smoothed
- 8–16 bars — a recognizable skeleton the learner can actually play this week

Famous pieces used in Month One: Beethoven *Ode to Joy* (Days 8, 9, 14),
Mozart *Twinkle* theme (Day 10), Beethoven *Fifth Symphony* motif (Day 11),
Haydn *Surprise Symphony* (Day 17), Mozart *Eine kleine Nachtmusik* (Day 18),
Beethoven *Für Elise* (Days 20, 30), Brahms *Lullaby* (Day 21), Bach *Minuet
in G* (Day 22), Pachelbel *Canon* progression (Day 24), *Amazing Grace*
(Day 26), Schumann *Träumerei* (Day 27), Brahms *Waltz Op. 39 No. 15* (Day 28).

## How to read the practice columns

- **Melody / exercise** — what the learner actually plays that day.
- **Length** — bars of notated music, and the approximate playing time at the
  intended slow practice tempo.
- **Level** — difficulty on the course's own 1–5 scale for Month One:
  1 = first-day easy, 2 = easy, 3 = gentle stretch, 4 = needs a few sittings,
  5 = end-of-month challenge.
- **Practice** — suggested time on the day's new material. The rest of the
  daily 20–30 minutes is warm-up and re-playing earlier favourites:
  5 minutes review before, ~5 minutes free play after.

## Skills covered by the end of Month One

- Correct posture, hand shape and five-finger positions in both hands
- Reading simple treble and bass staff notation
- Note values through eighth notes, rests, 4/4 and 3/4 time
- Chords: C, F, G, Am, Dm, Em — blocked and broken
- C and G major scales, one octave
- Legato/staccato, dynamics, phrasing, intro to the sustain pedal
- Recognizable simplified classics: Ode to Joy, Für Elise, Minuet in G,
  Pachelbel's Canon, a Brahms waltz

# Month Two (Days 31–60)

**Entry skills** (end of Month One): five-finger positions both hands, basic
treble/bass reading, note values through eighth notes, rests, 4/4 and 3/4,
chords C F G Am Dm Em (blocked and broken), C and G major scales,
legato/staccato, dynamics, phrasing basics, pedal introduction.

**Goal**: by Day 60 the learner reads and plays a short two-hand piece in C,
G or F major — or in A minor, the relative minor of C — with steady pulse,
basic phrasing, chord or broken-chord accompaniment, and sensible fingering.
The recital piece is Greensleeves in A minor: it reuses C major's key
signature, consolidates the month's dotted rhythms, 3/4 waltz accompaniment,
balance and phrasing, and adds one new reading skill, the G♯ accidental (the
raised 7th that points home to A). The month strengthens Month One rather
than jumping to intermediate level.

**Engine limits**: one time signature per lesson ([2,4]/[3,4]/[4,4]), no ties
across barlines and no pickup bars — Day 56 teaches the *concept* of ties in
words while exercises use long single notes.

## End-of-Month-Two outcomes

- Reads treble and bass via landmarks and intervals, without naming every note
- Plays C, G and F major scales (with F♯ and B♭) one octave, hands separately
- Uses primary chords and inversions in C, G, F; knows I–IV–V informally
- Accompanies with held chords, broken chords, Alberti bass and waltz patterns
- Handles dotted quarter–eighth rhythms; understands ties conceptually
- Plays short two-hand pieces with balance and phrasing; recital: Greensleeves

**Review criteria**: four correct relaxed repetitions; steady counting aloud;
start at ~50–60 BPM and increase only when comfortable; stop at pain or
persistent tension. Play Days 44, 51 and 57 are deliberately light and must
not block progress — optional challenge material is marked as such in the
lesson text.
