# Six Months to Piano — Months Four to Six Scope

This scope covers Days 91–180 for an adult learner practising about 20–30
minutes most days. It continues the reviewed Days 1–90 curriculum and works
backward from a realistic end-of-course outcome.

## The realistic Day 180 level

After six months, a consistent learner should be a **confident late beginner
or early elementary pianist**. In examination-language terms this is broadly
pre-Grade 1 to Grade 1 for an average independent learner, although repertoire
level, sight-reading level and technical level rarely advance at exactly the
same speed. A particularly consistent learner with teacher feedback may begin
some Grade 2 material, but Grade 2 must not be promised as the course norm.

By Day 180 the learner should be able to:

- read short two-hand music without writing every note name on the page
- keep going through a small mistake instead of restarting
- play in C, G, D, A and F major, plus A and E minor at an elementary level
- play one-octave major scales hands separately with reliable fingering; play
  selected contrary-motion or very slow hands-together patterns as optional work
- recognise and use I, IV, V, V7 and vi chords in familiar keys
- count 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 and 6/8, including pickups, ties, dotted rhythms and
  simple groups of sixteenth notes
- balance a singing melody over held chords, broken chords, Alberti bass or a
  quiet repeated-note accompaniment
- use legato, staccato, accents, dynamics, phrase shaping and basic pedal by ear
- analyse a new page for key, meter, form, patterns and difficult bars before
  playing it
- learn a short elementary piece independently over one or two weeks
- perform two contrasting short pieces, or one piece plus a simple lead-sheet
  arrangement, with a steady pulse and musical shape

The goal is musical independence and dependable elementary playing—not speed,
virtuosity or possession of a long list of famous titles.

## What kind of music is realistic?

### Comfortable core repertoire

The learner should manage eight- to thirty-two-bar melodies and elementary
two-hand pieces such as:

- folk tunes, hymns and traditional songs with melody plus chords or broken bass
- *Ode to Joy*, *Amazing Grace*, *When the Saints*, *Greensleeves* and similar
  tunes in complete simplified two-hand arrangements
- Schumann's *Melody*, Op. 68 No. 1, and comparable early teaching pieces
- Schumann's *Soldier's March*, Op. 68 No. 2, after structured preparation
- selected easy minuets, marches, waltzes and short Romantic character pieces
- a simple pop-style melody from chord symbols using I–V–vi–IV or similar loops

### Reachable with preparation

- Schumann's *Little Piece*, Op. 68 No. 5
- the easier portions of Bach/Petzold's *Minuet in G*
- a carefully simplified or shortened *Für Elise* opening
- a slow introductory sonatina theme or an easy Burgmüller-style study

### Stretch or future repertoire—not course promises

The authentic full *Träumerei*, complete *Für Elise*, *Moonlight Sonata* first
movement, Beethoven Fifth piano arrangement, *Clair de lune*, complete Clementi
Sonatina Op. 36 No. 1 and a performance-tempo Burgmüller *Arabesque* are beyond
the dependable six-month outcome for most self-taught adults. They may be used
for listening, score study, isolated excerpts or slow long-term projects.

## Engine prerequisites before generation

Do not generate lessons that describe notation the software cannot show or
play. Before building the affected days, add and test:

1. Pickup measures and complementary final measures in schema, validation,
   engraving and audio.
2. Ties within and across barlines, with one audible attack and a visible curve.
3. Sixteenth-note duration `0.25`, correct beams and 6/8 grouping.
4. Exercise-level key signatures so recurring sharps/flats need not be printed
   as accidentals on every note.
5. Per-event articulation and accent marks; dynamic changes inside an exercise.
6. Optional pedal marks only if they can be rendered accurately; otherwise keep
   pedal work in prose and listening drills.

Until a prerequisite exists, substitute a supported consolidation exercise and
state the limitation. Never fake a tie with two audible attacks, disguise a
full measure as a pickup, or teach sixteenths using relabelled eighth notes.

# Month Four — Fluency Beyond Five-Finger Positions (Days 91–120)

## Month Four outcome

The learner reads short phrases across changing hand positions, plays in A
major and E minor, understands simple sixteenth-note groups, and prepares
Schumann's authentic *Melody*, Op. 68 No. 1. Technique remains relaxed and
hands-separate scale work remains the norm.

# Month Five — Independence, Harmony and Style (Days 121–150)

## Month Five outcome

The learner hears and uses common chord functions, reads simple independent
lines, handles syncopation and minor-key colour, and learns Schumann's
authentic *Little Piece*, Op. 68 No. 5. The learner begins making musical
decisions rather than waiting for every instruction.

# Month Six — Becoming an Independent Pianist (Days 151–180)

## Month Six outcome

The learner applies the course skills to unfamiliar elementary music, builds a
personal practice method, and prepares a two-piece final recital. One piece is
chosen from the reviewed course repertoire; the other may be a lead-sheet
arrangement or a carefully selected new elementary piece.

Recommended core recital choices are Schumann *Melody*, *Soldier's March* or
*Little Piece*, a polished course arrangement, and the learner's own simple
arrangement. Burgmüller's *Arabesque* may be an optional stretch project, not
the sole graduation test.

## Day 180 assessment

Completion means the learner can demonstrate most of the following at a
comfortable tempo:

- maintain pulse and recover after a mistake
- read and prepare an unfamiliar eight-bar elementary excerpt
- play two major scales hands separately and one I–IV–V7–I cadence from memory
- count one 6/8 excerpt and one tied/pickup or simple-sixteenth excerpt
- balance melody and accompaniment in a two-hand piece
- explain the form and practice plan of a chosen score
- perform two contrasting selections with clear beginnings and endings

This is a diagnostic celebration, not an examination. A learner who needs
more time on one item should repeat the relevant week while continuing to make
music.

## Generation and audit rules

- Build only one month after its engine prerequisites and reviewed recital
  score have passed.
- Generate representative days first: 91, 98, 112, 120; 121, 140, 142, 150;
  151, 158, 170 and 180.
- Inspect notation, extracted PDF text and audio before filling the month.
- Keep most PDFs at two to four pages and most play-alongs under two minutes.
- Include a lighter day every six to seven days and never test a skill before
  its teaching day.
- Use authentic library scores for recital preparation; do not ask an AI to
  reproduce their notes.
- Verify all fingerings, historical references, licenses and public-domain
  claims before publishing.
- Label core, optional and stretch work clearly so ambitious repertoire does
  not become a hidden requirement.
